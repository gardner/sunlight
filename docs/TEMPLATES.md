Yes. The email should be **plain, narrow, low-friction, and boringly professional**. The goal is to make it easy for an OIA/LGOIMA officer to process without feeling like they are being ambushed.

Your website brief already says Sunlight sends recurring OIA/LGOIMA requests, receives material by reply email or unique upload link, preserves files and metadata with provenance, and does **not** automatically publish received files in Phase 0. That should be reflected in the email so authorities understand the workflow. 

The request should also align with existing NZ norms: the Ombudsman’s requester guide covers requests under either the OIA or LGOIMA, and Te Kawa Mataaho encourages proactive release of responses to OIA requests. ([Ombudsman NZ][1])

## Recommended subject line

```text
Official information request — responses completed in [Month YYYY] — Sunlight request [REQUEST_ID]
```

For local authorities:

```text
LGOIMA request — responses completed in [Month YYYY] — Sunlight request [REQUEST_ID]
```

For central government:

```text
OIA request — responses completed in [Month YYYY] — Sunlight request [REQUEST_ID]
```

## Recommended email template

```text
Kia ora,

I am writing on behalf of Open Data Limited’s Sunlight project.

This is a request for official information under the [Official Information Act 1982 / Local Government Official Information and Meetings Act 1987].

Please provide copies of the official information responses completed by [AGENCY NAME] during [MONTH YYYY].

For this request, “completed responses” means final response letters or emails sent to requesters during that month in response to OIA/LGOIMA requests.

Please include:

1. the final response letter or email sent to the requester;
2. any documents, datasets, attachments, schedules, or other material released with that response;
3. where the response or released material is already publicly available, the relevant URL is sufficient.

To reduce administrative burden, this request does not seek:

1. requester names, email addresses, phone numbers, postal addresses, signatures, or other requester contact details;
2. internal correspondence about how requests were handled;
3. duplicate copies of material that is already publicly available, if a URL can be provided.

Please apply any redactions or withholding grounds you consider necessary before release, including for privacy or other statutory reasons.

Our preferred format is electronic. Please reply to this email with the material attached, or use the secure upload link below for larger files:

[UNIQUE_UPLOAD_LINK]

If it would be easier to provide a spreadsheet or schedule first, please provide whatever fields are readily available, such as:

- agency reference number;
- response date;
- request subject or title;
- outcome;
- withholding grounds used, if any;
- public URL, if already published.

Sunlight collects and preserves official information response material so it can be reviewed, searched, and reused responsibly by future public-interest tools. Material received by Sunlight is not automatically published.

You can verify this request or contact us here:

[PUBLIC_VERIFICATION_URL]
[CONTACT_EMAIL]

Ngā mihi,

[NAME]
Open Data Limited / Sunlight
[WEBSITE]
[REQUEST_ID]
```

## Slightly softer version for first contact

For the first few months, I’d use a softer wording that gives agencies an easy way to respond with an index or URLs first:

```text
Kia ora,

I am writing on behalf of Open Data Limited’s Sunlight project.

This is a request for official information under the [Official Information Act 1982 / Local Government Official Information and Meetings Act 1987].

Please provide copies of the official information responses completed by [AGENCY NAME] during [MONTH YYYY], where these can be provided without substantial collation.

For this request, “completed responses” means final response letters or emails sent to requesters during that month in response to OIA/LGOIMA requests.

If providing the full response material would be difficult, please provide a schedule or export of the completed responses instead, including any fields readily available, such as:

- agency reference number;
- response date;
- request subject or title;
- outcome;
- withholding grounds used, if any;
- public URL, if already published.

Where responses or released documents are already publicly available, URLs are sufficient.

This request does not seek requester names, contact details, signatures, or internal correspondence about how requests were handled. Please apply any redactions or withholding grounds you consider necessary before release.

Our preferred format is electronic. Please reply to this email or use the secure upload link below for larger files:

[UNIQUE_UPLOAD_LINK]

Sunlight collects and preserves official information response material so it can be reviewed, searched, and reused responsibly by future public-interest tools. Material received by Sunlight is not automatically published.

You can verify this request or contact us here:

[PUBLIC_VERIFICATION_URL]
[CONTACT_EMAIL]

Ngā mihi,

[NAME]
Open Data Limited / Sunlight
[WEBSITE]
[REQUEST_ID]
```

## My recommendation

Use the **softer version first**.

It is less likely to trigger an immediate “substantial collation” refusal, while still asking for the thing you actually want. Once you learn which agencies have tidy OIA registers and proactive-release processes, you can tighten the request later.

## One important wording choice

Avoid saying:

```text
Please send us all OIAs.
```

Say:

```text
Please provide copies of official information responses completed during [MONTH YYYY].
```

That is clearer, less aggressive, and more administratively understandable.

## Optional footer

Add this to every email:

```text
About Sunlight:
Sunlight is a public-interest project. It collects and preserves official information response material for future public-interest review, search, and responsible reuse. Sunlight is independent and is not a government service.
```

That matches the public positioning in the Sunlight website brief and reassures authorities that this is not a random anonymous data grab.
