# Open Data Public Web Design Brief

## Purpose

This document is a creative brief and prompt source for generating landing page design directions in Stitch and Claude Design.

The goal is to generate strong visual directions for the **OpenData.org.nz** public landing page.

Open Data is the parent public-interest organisation behind projects such as **Sunlight**. The website should explain the wider mission, establish trust, and provide a clear foundation for future open data, transparency, archival, civic technology, research, and public-interest infrastructure projects.

This is not the implementation specification. It is a design and messaging prompt.

---

## Typography Research

Use **Fira Sans** as the primary typeface.

Rationale:

* Fira Sans is readable, open source, and already familiar in New Zealand public-service contexts.
* It feels clear and practical without looking too formal or bureaucratic.
* Open Data should feel civic and trustworthy, but it should not mimic an official government website.

Recommended CSS stack:

```css
font-family: "Fira Sans", Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif;
```

Optional typographic direction:

* Use Fira Sans for all interface, body, and navigation text.
* Consider a slightly stronger weight for headings rather than introducing a separate display typeface.
* Prioritise readability, directness, and long-term maintainability.

## Design Direction

**Open Data** should feel:

* independent
* charitable / public-interest
* civic
* practical
* trustworthy
* technically competent
* calm
* open
* durable

The site should communicate that Open Data is building long-term public infrastructure, not launching a single campaign or product.

It should not look like:

* a government department
* a political campaign
* a generic SaaS landing page
* a startup pitch deck
* a crypto / “radical transparency” hype site
* a legal-services brochure
* an academic institution
* a corporate data consultancy

Avoid New Zealand government visual signals:

* no coat of arms
* no flags as major graphic devices
* no Beehive imagery as a hero image
* no official government green/black palette
* no dense bureaucratic header treatment

Use an independent civic palette. Good directions include:

* warm white
* ink
* deep teal
* muted blue
* restrained yellow
* clay / muted coral
* soft grey
* paper tones
* quiet green accents, used carefully

The visual system should suggest:

* openness
* access
* records
* public knowledge
* civic infrastructure
* careful stewardship
* data made legible

Use visual motifs such as:

* grids
* records
* open folders
* structured data
* document trails
* archives
* civic maps
* linked datasets
* provenance lines
* simple diagrams
* search/index patterns

Avoid:

* decorative gradient blobs
* empty marketing illustrations
* oversized SaaS cards
* generic stock photos
* fake dashboards
* overly futuristic AI imagery
* dramatic “exposing secrets” visuals

Open Data should feel like a serious public-interest organisation that builds useful infrastructure.

## Core Message

Open Data exists to make important data and public information **open, free, available, searchable, and reusable**.

It builds and operates projects that help people find, understand, preserve, and reuse public-interest information.

The first major project is **Sunlight**, which collects and preserves official information response material for future public-interest review, search, and reuse.

---

## Information Architecture

The landing page should support these audiences:

* members of the public who want to understand the mission
* journalists and researchers
* civic technologists and open data practitioners
* potential funders and supporters
* public agencies and authorities
* future collaborators
* people looking for projects such as Sunlight

Recommended top navigation:

* Projects

* Support

Optional if more space is needed:

Primary page sections:

1. Header
2. Hero

## Page Sections

### 1. Header

The header should include:

* Open Data wordmark or text identity
* concise navigation
* clear link to Projects
* clear link to Contact or Support

Suggested navigation:

* About
* Projects
* Principles
* Support
* Contact

Avoid clutter. Open Data should feel calm and established, not busy.

---

### 2. Hero

The hero should make the name and mission clear in the first viewport.

Suggested headline:

**Open Data**

Suggested supporting copy:

**Open Data builds public-interest infrastructure to make important data and official information open, free, searchable, and reusable.**

Alternative supporting copy:

**We help collect, preserve, organise, and publish public-interest data so it can be found, understood, and used by everyone.**

Primary CTA:

**View projects**

Secondary CTA:

**Read our principles**

The hero should make clear:

* Open Data is independent.
* It is public-interest focused.
* It builds infrastructure and projects.
* It is not a government service.
* It does not exist to sell proprietary data products.

---

### 3. Mission

Section heading:

**Our mission**

Suggested copy:

Open Data exists to make important information easier to access, understand, and reuse.

Public-interest data should not be locked away in scattered files, broken links, private inboxes, or systems that only specialists can use. We build tools, archives, and processes that help keep useful information available for public benefit.

Our work supports journalism, research, civic technology, education, accountability, and better public understanding.

---

### 4. What Open Data Does

Use simple, concrete cards or rows.

Suggested items:

#### Collect

We collect public-interest data, official information, public records, and related material.

#### Preserve

We keep source material, metadata, and provenance together so records remain useful over time.

#### Organise

We structure information so it can be searched, linked, compared, and reused.

#### Publish

We prepare public-interest information for responsible public access where lawful and appropriate.

#### Build

We create tools, archives, search systems, APIs, and civic technology projects.

#### Explain

We publish plain-language material that helps people understand how public information systems work.

---

### 5. Projects

Section heading:

**Projects**

This section should present Open Data as the umbrella organisation and show that projects sit underneath it.

Suggested intro copy:

Open Data runs public-interest projects that improve access to data and official information.

Featured project:

#### Sunlight

Sunlight collects and preserves official information response material from public authorities so it can be reviewed, searched, and reused responsibly by future public-interest tools.

CTA:

**Visit Sunlight**

Possible future project placeholder:

#### More projects coming

Open Data will support future projects focused on public records, civic datasets, research tools, and open information infrastructure.

Avoid overpromising. Do not imply future projects already exist unless they do.

---

### 6. Why It Matters

Section heading:

**Why open data matters**

Suggested copy:

Useful public information is often fragmented. It may be spread across agency websites, email responses, PDFs, spreadsheets, archives, and databases that are difficult to search or reuse.

When information is hard to find, it becomes less useful. Journalists, researchers, communities, developers, and the public lose time reconstructing what should already be accessible.

Open Data helps reduce that fragmentation by building shared infrastructure for public-interest information.

Possible supporting points:

* Better access supports better public understanding.
* Preserved records reduce duplication and loss.
* Searchable archives help journalists and researchers work faster.
* Open tools help civic technologists build on public information.
* Clear provenance makes information easier to verify.

---

### 7. Principles

Section heading:

**Our principles**

Use plain-language principle cards.

#### Open by default

We aim to make public-interest data freely available where lawful, responsible, and practical.

#### Provenance matters

People should be able to see where information came from and how it has been handled.

#### Public benefit first

Our work is designed to support public understanding, research, journalism, civic technology, and accountability.

#### Careful handling

Not all information should be published automatically. Privacy, safety, legal obligations, and context matter.

#### Reuse is the goal

Data becomes more valuable when people can search it, cite it, build on it, and use it responsibly.

#### Independent and non-partisan

Open Data is independent. It is not a government service, political party, or campaign organisation.

---

### 8. How We Work

Section heading:

**How we work**

Suggested copy:

Open Data develops projects that collect, preserve, organise, and make public-interest information easier to use.

Our work may include:

* building archives and search tools
* collecting public records and official information
* preserving files and metadata
* creating public datasets
* developing civic technology
* publishing research and explainers
* supporting responsible reuse through clear provenance and open access

Where material may involve privacy, sensitivity, or legal risk, we use review and handling processes before publication.

---

### 9. Support / Collaborate

Section heading:

**Support the work**

Suggested copy:

Open Data is building long-term public-interest infrastructure. We welcome support from people and organisations who care about open information, public records, civic technology, research, journalism, and democratic accountability.

Ways to help:

* contribute technical expertise
* suggest datasets or public-interest problems
* support a project
* collaborate on research or tooling
* help with governance, law, privacy, archives, or data stewardship
* contact us about funding or partnership opportunities

CTA:

**Contact Open Data**

Avoid saying “donate” unless donation handling is ready. Avoid saying “registered charity” unless registration is complete.

---

### 10. Footer

Footer should include:

* Contact
* Privacy
* Projects
* Governance or legal status
* Project status
* Open Data Limited legal name

Suggested footer note:

**Open Data is an independent public-interest organisation. It is not a government service.**

If charity registration is not complete, avoid “registered charity.”

Possible legal wording:

**Open Data is operated by Open Data Limited, a strictly non-profit company established for public-interest open data work.**

Once charity registration is approved, this can become:

**Open Data is operated by Open Data Limited, a registered charitable company in New Zealand.**

## Copy Direction

Use plain, direct language.

Prefer:

* “public-interest data”
* “official information”
* “open data”
* “public records”
* “searchable archives”
* “free and reusable”
* “clear provenance”
* “responsible publication”
* “public benefit”

Avoid:

* “data liberation”
* “unlocking truth”
* “radical transparency”
* “democratising everything”
* “AI-powered revolution”
* “trustless infrastructure”
* “information superhighway”
* “disrupting government”
* “decentralised public knowledge layer”

The tone should be professional and calm.

It should be more expansive than Sunlight, but still concrete. Open Data is the higher mission; Sunlight is one project underneath it.

Do not overpromise:

* do not imply all data will be free of legal or practical limits
* do not imply every dataset can be published
* do not imply public search exists before it does
* do not imply Open Data is a government service
* do not imply Open Data is already a registered charity unless registration is complete
* do not imply automated AI systems make final publication decisions

## Evaluation Criteria

When comparing generated designs, prefer the direction that:

* makes the Open Data mission immediately clear
* feels broader than Sunlight while still related to it
* feels independent but institutionally credible
* explains public benefit without becoming vague or ideological
* clearly presents Sunlight as a project under Open Data
* uses accessible contrast and readable typography
* avoids government mimicry
* avoids SaaS/startup marketing tropes
* avoids overpromising public search, upload, or charity status
* can be implemented cleanly as a simple landing page
* feels durable enough for a parent organisation that may run multiple future projects
